# UGC ad scripts

Write scripts that a real creator can film on a phone — not polished brand copy read to camera.
The output is paste-ready: what to say, what appears on screen, and how to deliver it.

## When to use

- A UGC ad or creator script, a short-form vertical ad, a founder-led video.
- Employee-generated content or a customer-testimonial script.

Not for scoring existing hooks, writing static ad copy, or briefing a designer.

## Inputs to gather

- The product and **one thing it visibly does**.
- The buyer and their exact words about the problem — reviews, comments, forum posts. Copy phrases
  word for word; invented customer language reads fake.
- Placement (organic, paid social, marketplace) and what claims are provable.

## Structure of each concept

1. **Hook (0–3 s)** — a pattern interrupt or a problem stated in the buyer's own words.
2. **Body** — the product shown doing the thing, one idea, no feature dump.
3. **CTA** — one clear next step, matched to the placement.

Write **3–5 hook variants per concept**, and for each hook give three agreeing layers:

- **Spoken line** — short, conversational, around 6–8 words for a 3-second opener.
- **On-screen text** — the same promise in few words, inside the platform safe zone.
- **Opening visual** — exactly what is in frame at 0:00.

The three must agree. A hook only has to stop the right people — relevance beats a big open.

## Delivery notes

Add how to say it: pace, tone, a pause, a laugh, a slight imperfection. UGC works because it feels
unscripted; over-rehearsed reads kill it. Note the framing (casual, handheld, first-person) and any
B-roll that shows the product working.

## Compliance

- Include disclosure lines where an ad, sponsorship or paid partnership must be labelled.
- No health, income or outcome claims that cannot be substantiated.
- Use only testimonials and quotes the user has the right to use; mark any placeholder clearly.

## Rules

- Never invent a customer quote and present it as real — label it a draft line to be replaced.
- Match the format to the placement: B2C tends to problem→reveal; B2B often leads with a specific
  outcome or a critique of the old way.
- Give the creator a shot list, not just a script — the footage needs to be filmable.
- No auto-generation of a video; this skill writes the script the creator films or another skill
  produces.
