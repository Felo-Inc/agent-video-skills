# Product video hooks

The hook is the first two to three seconds, and it decides whether anyone sees the rest. A hook
only has to stop the **right** people, and it must lead into the product — a clever open that does
not set up the product wastes the impression.

## Inputs (ask once)

- The product and the **one thing it visibly does**.
- The buyer and their exact words about the problem — copy phrases word for word from reviews,
  comments or forums.
- Where it runs (organic, marketplace, paid social) and what claims are provable.

## Write each hook as three layers

| Layer | What it is | Example (knee sleeve) |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| Visual (0:00) | What is in the first frame | A woman stops halfway up the stairs, hand on her knee |
| Spoken | One line, ~6–8 words, fits about 3 s | "Knees crack on the stairs? Watch this." |
| Text overlay | The same promise in ≤8 words, inside the safe zone | "Knees crack on stairs? See fix" |

The three must agree. Better relevance with a smaller audience beats a broad open that attracts the
wrong viewers and drops retention.

## Hook types to draw from

Late discovery, compliment then reveal, problem and fix in one clip, reply to a comment, bold claim
then proof, before/after, "things I wish I knew",POV, and counterintuitive takes. Match the type to
the buyer's awareness stage:

- **Unaware** — lead with the problem in their words.
- **Problem-aware** — lead with the fix and a quick demonstration.
- **Solution-aware / most aware** — lead with the differentiator or proof.

## Checks before delivery

- Does the hook lead into the product within the first few seconds?
- Are the visual, spoken line and overlay saying the same thing?
- Does every claim hold up under the platform's rules? No invented outcomes or guaranteed results.
- Is the spoken line short enough to fit three seconds at a natural pace?
- Is the overlay inside the platform safe zone and legible at phone size?

When footage exists and the user approves, render one short MP4 per hook so they can test opens
against each other. Do not publish anything automatically.

## Rules

- Never fabricate a statistic, endorsement or customer quote.
- Do not reuse a hook from a competitor's ad verbatim — take the structure, write original copy.
- Give several genuinely different hooks, not one idea reworded.
- A strong opening with weak retention is not a win; say so if the body does not pay off the promise.
